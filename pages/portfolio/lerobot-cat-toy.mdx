  <h2 style={{ fontSize: '1.8rem', color: '#0056b3', borderBottom: '2px solid #e0e0e0', paddingBottom: '0.5rem', marginBottom: '1.5rem' }}>Project Overview</h2>
  <p style={{ fontSize: '1.1rem', lineHeight: '1.8', marginBottom: '2rem', color: '#333' }}>
    While cat-sitting for my partner's two cats, I built an SO-101 follower arm by hand following Hugging Face's official build guide, and wrote a script on top of their LeRobot library to generate randomized motions for a cat toy to keep them entertained. What started as a way to keep two bored cats happy turned into a small robotics exercise in its own right: forward kinematics, collision checking against the table, and watching the arm's live behavior in a meshcat viewer (no pun intended) before ever trusting it to move on its own near the cats.
  </p>
  <p style={{ fontSize: '1.1rem', lineHeight: '1.8', marginBottom: '2rem', color: '#333' }}>
    The script itself loads the SO-101's URDF model and uses placo to compute forward kinematics for the arm's current joint state, so it can check every candidate move against the tabletop before executing it. The meshcat viewer mirrors the robot alongside a plane-restricted zone above the table, making it easy to confirm a planned motion stays clear of the surface before it ever runs on the real arm. From a manually-set home pose, motions are randomized between specific joint-angle bounds, mixing slow "glides" with quicker "darts" while avoiding joint moves that are too large a jump to be safe, streaming commands to the real arm at 30 FPS.
  </p>
  <p style={{ fontSize: '1.1rem', lineHeight: '1.8', marginBottom: '2rem', color: '#333' }}>
    One of the more interesting findings was mechanical rather than software: adding an "8th axis" by attaching a flexible rod cat toy (the kind normally held by a human) to the gripper with a flexible rubber-band connection turned out to be the best way to translate the energy of the arm's joint motion directly into motion at the toy's tip, rather than the rigid connection I first tried.
  </p>

  <h2 style={{ fontSize: '1.8rem', color: '#0056b3', borderBottom: '2px solid #e0e0e0', paddingBottom: '0.5rem', marginBottom: '1.5rem' }}>Key Contributions</h2>
  <ul style={{ fontSize: '1.1rem', lineHeight: '1.8', marginBottom: '2rem', color: '#333', paddingLeft: '1.5rem' }}>
    <li style={{ marginBottom: '1rem' }}><strong>Hardware Build:</strong> Assembled and wired the SO-101 follower arm from Hugging Face's official build guide.</li>
    <li style={{ marginBottom: '1rem' }}><strong>Forward Kinematics & Collision Checking:</strong> Used the SO-101 URDF with placo to compute forward kinematics for the arm, checking every candidate motion against a plane-restricted zone above the table before executing it.</li>
    <li style={{ marginBottom: '1rem' }}><strong>Bounded Randomized Motion:</strong> Wrote a motion generator that randomizes joint states within specific angle bounds, mixing slow glides and quick darts while avoiding overly large joint moves that could be unsafe.</li>
    <li style={{ marginBottom: '1rem' }}><strong>Live Visualization:</strong> Used a live meshcat viewer showing the robot and its restricted zone above the table, with a one-shot pose-check mode for calibrating joint offsets before trusting the arm near the cats.</li>
    <li style={{ marginBottom: '1rem' }}><strong>End-Effector Tuning:</strong> Found that adding a flexible rubber-band connection between the gripper and a flexible rod cat toy (rather than a rigid one) translated joint motion much more effectively into motion at the toy's tip.</li>
    <li style={{ marginBottom: '1rem' }}><strong>Safe Operation:</strong> Built in a hardcoded home pose and safety behavior so the arm holds position (torque on) when stopped, rather than dropping or continuing to move unexpectedly.</li>
  </ul>
