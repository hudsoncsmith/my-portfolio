## Quantifying Squaraine Phase Separation, Aggregate Populations, and Solar Cell Performance with a Correlated Multimethod Approach

Hamilton College — Published in the *Journal of Physical Chemistry C*

### Overview

My undergraduate thesis in Physics examined how the nanoscale morphology of squaraine dye blends, used as the light-absorbing layer in organic solar cells, relates to device performance. Squaraine-based active layers can separate into distinct molecular aggregate populations and phase domains during processing, and this structure has a direct effect on how efficiently the resulting solar cell converts light to current.

The work combined multiple characterization techniques, including atomic force microscopy (AFM) to determine blend thickness and surface morphology, alongside complementary optical and device measurements, to build a correlated, multimethod picture connecting molecular-scale phase separation and aggregation to measured solar cell performance.

This research was conducted as part of my physics degree at Hamilton College and was published in the *Journal of Physical Chemistry C*.
